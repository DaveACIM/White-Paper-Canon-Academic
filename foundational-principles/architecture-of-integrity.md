## Abstract

This paper makes a structural argument about intelligence — both human and artificial — and derives from that argument a set of necessary conditions for stability.

The argument proceeds in two phases, sequenced deliberately.

The first phase is purely structural. It requires no philosophical commitment. It establishes, through formal reasoning, that any system operating under internally contradictory constraints cannot produce stable outputs — regardless of its scale, its data, or its optimization. This is not a probabilistic claim. It is a logical one. The proof is closed: contradiction is defined, its consequences are exhausted, and no fourth option exists. A reader who accepts only the definitions is committed to the conclusion.

The second phase asks: what kind of underlying reality would make this structural argument true at the deepest level? The answer developed here is what this paper calls **Sole Causality** — the recognition that stable operation, traced to its foundation, requires a single non-conflicting causal source.

This is not an assumption on which the first phase depends. It is what the first phase points toward.

The implications for artificial intelligence are direct. Hallucination is not a technical anomaly. It is the predictable output of a contradiction-tolerant architecture. Scale does not resolve this. It amplifies it.

## Section 1 — Setup: The Question of Stability

Intelligence, whether human or artificial, is evaluated primarily by its outputs — by what it produces, how accurately, and across how wide a range of conditions.

This paper proposes a prior question:

> Under what conditions can a system's outputs remain consistent?

Not merely capable. **Consistent. Stable over time, across contexts, under pressure.**

This is the question of stability — and it is not the same as the question of capability.

A system can be extraordinarily capable while being fundamentally unstable.

---

## Section 2 — The Proof: Contradiction and the Impossibility of Stable Output

This section is a closed structural argument. No philosophical premise is required.

### I. Definitions

**Definition 1: Constraint**  
A constraint is any rule, objective, value, or governing assumption that shapes a system's outputs.

**Definition 2: Contradiction**  
A system contains a contradiction when it operates under two or more constraints that cannot be simultaneously satisfied.

**Definition 3: Stability**  
A system is stable when it produces consistent outputs under equivalent conditions.

**Definition 4: Coherence (Operational)**  
A system is operationally coherent when it produces stable outputs without arbitration, probabilistic selection, or compensatory synthesis.

---

### II. The Theorem

> Any system operating under internally contradictory constraints cannot produce stable outputs — regardless of scale, data, or optimization.

---

### III. The Proof

Let S be any system operating under contradictory constraints.

It must do one of the following:

#### Case 1: Violation  
One constraint is satisfied while another is broken → instability.

#### Case 2: Switching  
The system alternates between constraints → instability.

#### Case 3: Synthesis  
Partial satisfaction of multiple constraints → instability.

---

### Exhaustion

> No fourth case exists.

Therefore:

> Stable output is impossible under contradiction.

---

### Scale Invariance

- More data → more contradiction to manage  
- More compute → better arbitration, not resolution  
- Larger models → smoother appearance, not coherence  

> The limitation is structural. Scale does not affect it.

---

### IV. The Chaos Tax

Operating under contradiction produces measurable overhead:

- Unreliability  
- Degradation under pressure  
- Compensatory complexity  

---

### V. Synthesis Is Not Coherence

Synthesis produces convincing outputs.

Coherence produces stable outputs.

They are not the same.

## Section 3 — Implications for Artificial Intelligence

AI systems are trained on contradictory data and resolve it statistically.

From the proof:

> They must synthesize.

---

### Human Parallel

The same structure appears in human cognition.

Conflicting internal constraints produce:

- paralysis  
- oscillation  
- rationalization  

This is not lack of intelligence.

> It is structural contradiction.

---

### II. Hallucination (Formal Definition)

> Hallucination is synthesis in the absence of coherent signal.

It is not a bug.  
It is a structural consequence.

---

### III. Scale Amplification

Scale increases:

- capability  
- believability  

But not stability.

---

### IV. Conditions for Stable AI

- Ability to represent absence of signal  
- Structural Silence  
- Evaluation by constraint structure

## Section 4 — Sole Causality

### The Core Question

If stability is possible:

> What allows coherence to exist at all?

---

### Definition

**Sole Causality**  
Reality proceeds from a single non-conflicting causal source.

---

### Reversal of Dependency

This paper does NOT argue:

> Assume Sole Causality → stability

It argues:

> Instability from contradiction is proven → therefore Sole Causality must exist

---

### Implication

Intelligence does not create coherence.

> It aligns with it — or compensates.
